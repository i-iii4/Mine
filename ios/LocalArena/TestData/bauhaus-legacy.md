The Bauhaus school, founded in 1919 by Walter Gropius in Weimar, Germany, fundamentally changed how we think about the relationship between form and function. Its principles continue to shape digital interface design a century later.

## Grid Systems

The typographic grid, pioneered by Bauhaus designers like Herbert Bayer and Jan Tschichold, is the foundation of every modern layout system. From Bootstrap to CSS Grid, we build on ideas first articulated in the 1920s.

![Bauhaus grid system](bauhaus-grid.svg)

## Color Theory

Josef Albers' "Interaction of Color" remains essential reading for anyone working with digital palettes. His experiments with simultaneous contrast directly inform how we choose accessible color combinations.

![Color interaction study](bauhaus-color.svg)

## Typography as Interface

Bauhaus rejected ornamental typography in favor of clarity. This same principle drives the current trend toward geometric sans-serif typefaces in UI design: Inter, SF Pro, Geist.

The school's legacy is not just aesthetic. It's the idea that design is a systematic discipline, not decoration — that every element must serve a purpose.
